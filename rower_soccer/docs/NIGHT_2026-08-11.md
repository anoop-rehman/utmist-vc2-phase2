## The big finding: the kick drill never paid the ant to kick

Six arms across four hypotheses all failed, and the cause turned out to be a
decision recorded in our own design notes. Section 2 forced `w_strike = 0` under
the timed reward, arguing "power is a consequence of the deadline". It isn't:
**arrival is paid once, at T; the approach shaping is paid every step.** Walking
at the ball outweighs where the ball ends up.

Measured on v9, 2703 closed segments, per-segment peak ball speed:

| | |
|---|---|
| median | **0.00 m/s** |
| never really struck (<1 m/s) | **63.6%** |
| in the 6-9 m/s useful band | 1.3% |
| overshoot | 0.0% |

Not striking is an *attractor*, which is why every timed arm **declined** rather
than plateaued (v6: 0.1288 → 0.0974 over 100M). With nothing striking, aim was
unconstrained and drifted to *worse than chance*.

**The decoder result is real but narrower than I first said.** Unfreezing the
`follow` decoder is the only thing that has ever moved aim off the random
baseline — v9 74.1° against v8's 134.2° at three times the training, with
training time excluded in the strongest way (frozen arms get *worse* with more
steps). But an arm that reaches useful power in 1.3% of segments cannot express
good aim as an outcome. **Aim was necessary; a reason to strike is also
necessary; no arm has ever had both.**

### Decision 1 — the NPMP fork

v9 breaks the shared-decoder arrangement by construction. If a directed strike
is outside what a `follow`-trained decoder can represent, the design can't hold
as-is:

- **Train the decoder on a striking task too** — keeps shared-`z`; costs a
  redesign of the decoder stage and a re-run of everything downstream.
- **Per-skill decoders** — cheapest; discards the shared-`z` the pipeline is
  built on, so the 2v2 controller switches between four unrelated controllers
  rather than steering one.
- **Unfreeze during drills with a KL anchor to the follow decoder** — middle
  path, bounds gait drift, untested here.

### Decision 2 — restore `w_strike` under `timed`?

Recommended, not launched. It reverses a documented decision, and a seventh arm
before the fork above is resolved risks answering a question about a pipeline
we may be about to change.

## D2 (CompetEvo) — stages 3 and 3b landed

Two independent learners plus a bounded opponent-checkpoint ring, then the
opponent forwards collapsed into one stacked matmul. Gates re-run by me: 6/6
then 11/11. The batching also removed a discarded opponent critic and 24
device→host syncs per step. Whole-iteration win **1.57x** (noisy, 1.35-1.92x) —
much smaller than the 33.5x on the isolated section, and reported as such.

An agent is running **2e, paper-number validation**, which the corrected
throughput makes an overnight job rather than a multi-day one.

Their `delta` turned out to be a **window, not a mixing probability**, and their
`randint` is high-exclusive, so P(facing the current opponent) is **0**, not the
0.5 I had briefed. We implement theirs.

## Errors I made and corrected

Recorded because the pattern matters more than the individual slips — every one
was a *derived* number that looked plausible and went unchecked against a second
measurement.

| error | caught by | correction |
|---|---|---|
| CompetEvo port "168 env-steps/s, slower than CPU" | an agent | `sec` is cumulative, not per-iteration — 20x wrong; it's 3,505 |
| `best.pt` inflated 1.6x | an agent | compared a deterministic eval against a training-monitor number; ~1.1x |
| dribble "0.77-0.79" | me | single monitor lines off an aliased oscillation; mean is 0.607 |
| v7 anchor "ahead of control" | me | mismatched step counts; identical at matched steps |
| "longer deadlines are worse" | me | confounded — T = dist/pace, so long-T buckets have farther targets |
| strike speed "1.68 m/s" | me | probe averaged the ball's deceleration tail, not strikes |
| v9 "evidence against the decoder" | me | aim leads fitness; I applied that to v8 then forgot it for v9 |

The habits that caught these are now written into the notes: wall-clock
cross-check every derived rate, compare arms at matched steps, quote means not
single samples, and verify a probe measures the quantity you think it does
before reporting it.
