# Status report — 2026-08-08

*Mini-report in the style of a supervisor update: what this project is, where it
stands, what we learned this week, and what happens next. Companion technical
deep-dive: [NPMP_SMP_POSTMORTEM.md](NPMP_SMP_POSTMORTEM.md).*

## What this project is

Deliver the video promised three years ago as a design-team club project:
**2v2 creature soccer**, each team one 8-DOF two-arm rower + one 2-DOF
three-segment worm — GA-evolved Unity creatures ported to MuJoCo — playing
recognizable soccer. Method: DeepMind's humanoid-football pipeline in miniature
(drills → distilled priors → human demos via BC → KL-anchored self-play);
see [PIPELINE_V2.md](PIPELINE_V2.md) for the stage roadmap.

## Decision record — 2026-08-08 pivot

**Locomotion-style mimicry is dropped as a goal.** The original motivation for
gait imitation was a fear that the rower was stuck in a "not moving" local
optimum. That was diagnosed (July) as the `gear_scale` bug — the rower had the
worm's 0.03 and physically could not move — not an optimization problem. With
it fixed, plain task RL solves the drills. The creatures may move however they
want; the only gates are task completion and a watchable final video.

Consequences:
- The NPMP/SMP motor-prior line is **parked**, not deleted (postmortem doc).
- The style metric (`rower_soccer/tools/style.py`) stays as a free diagnostic
  (logged as `eval/style` in follow training) but gates nothing.
- The sprint is: remaining drills → BC → self-play → heterogeneous 2v2 video.

## What this week established (condensed)

1. **Machine migration + disk resize completed**; stack verified byte-compatible
   (torque gate reproduces the exact 1.98x margin across mujoco 3.1.3 → 3.11).
2. **The lost NPMP decoder was retrained better than the original** (joint
   error 8.0° vs 11.7°) in 62 min, and is safely in GCS.
3. **Fitness is blind to motion style** — measured, not suspected: a policy
   scoring 0.950 fitness can be a visually-twitching block (style 0.30) or a
   rowing creature (July original). Built and validated a 4-term style metric
   to quantify this; 13 adversarial self-tests.
4. **Frozen-decoder NPMP transfer does not preserve style** (5-arm ablation,
   all arms 0.34-0.41 vs the decoder's own 0.93). Root insight: nothing in a
   task objective makes the latent oscillate at gait frequency. Detail in the
   postmortem. This line is now moot for the project but is a real negative
   result worth keeping.
5. **SMP/MimicKit reproduction was stood up on our Warp/Newton stack** and was
   tracking the paper's training curve when killed by the pivot (also in the
   postmortem, resumable).
6. **Follow-task performance was never the problem**: five independent runs hit
   fitness 0.92-0.97 in ~50 min each.

**Throughput** (RTX 4000 Ada pod, Xeon E5-2650 v4 @ 2.2 GHz — *slower
single-thread than any current laptop, so these are lower bounds for a laptop
host*). Realtime = 40 Hz control:

| config | ms/control step | control Hz | × realtime |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2v2 ant, physics_dt 0.0025 (drill-matched) | 9.0 | 111 | **2.8x** |
| 2v2 ant, physics_dt 0.005 (soccer native) | 6.2 | 162 | 4.1x |
| 2v2 ant + 640×480 top-down render, no shadows | 13.9 | 72 | **1.8x** |
| 1 ant, physics_dt 0.0025 | 5.1 | 197 | 4.9x |

So a laptop hosts the authoritative sim *and* server-side rendering with
headroom; no pod + port-forward needed for physics reasons. (Shadows/MSAA must
stay off — the pitch ships four 8192² shadowmaps, ~100 ms/frame.)

**Sim2sim (the WS4 blocker), verdict: CPU dm_control game as planned.** Full
numbers in ANT_SPRINT_WORKSTREAMS.md. Headline: `follow_ant_v1` scores median
fitness **0.892** in the CPU dm_control drill against **0.905** in warp, from
matched initial states, with the two root trajectories **2.5-12.5 cm apart
after a full 15 s on every episode** — even though the same action sequence
replayed open-loop separates by metres. Closed-loop feedback absorbs the contact
difference on a statically stable body, which is exactly the property the ant
was chosen for.

Two things the probe turned up that are *not* sim2sim:

- **An observation-contract bug** (fixed): the warp envs scale the
  accelerometer `/100` and clip to ±50; `creature.py` returned it raw, so every
  CPU-side eval fed the policy a different vector. Worth fitness 0.89 → 0.28.
- **An absorbing "sit down" fixed point in `follow_ant_v1`** (open): present in
  both backends (1 of 6 probe seeds, reproduced identically in each) and
  *deterministic* when the commanded target is within ~1° of dead ahead — i.e.
  precisely what a human clicking "go there" produces. Mitigation today is ~3°
  of bearing dither in the SkillController; a stationary-target training arm is
  the real fix.

## Sprint plan (proposed order)

1. **Dribble to gate** on the rower (and worm), unconstrained motion, using the
   existing dribble env + the follow policy as warm start where it helps.
2. **Kick + shoot** as env variants of dribble (ball interaction machinery
   already exists; these are reward/termination variants more than new envs).
3. **Warp 2v2 soccer env** — the one real engineering unknown. Two options to
   evaluate at build time: extend `warp_port/scene.py` to two-team multi-creature
   worlds (fastest path given all drill machinery is warp-native), or accept the
   CPU dm_control env for the final low-throughput self-play stage only.
4. **BC from human demos** (stage 4-5) or — cheaper first pass — skip straight
   to **self-play from drill-warm-started policies** and see if watchable play
   emerges without demos. The 2019 DeepMind result (below) suggests it can.
5. **Heterogeneous 2v2, render the video, done.**

## Cost/time estimates (asked 2026-08-08)

Reference prices (RunPod, on-demand, Aug 2026): RTX 4090 ~$0.34/hr community /
$0.69 secure; A100 80G from ~$1.39/hr; H100 from ~$2.89/hr. Current pod
(RTX 4000 Ada) is in the ~$0.3/hr class. Billing per-second.

**Anchor throughputs (measured on this pod):** single-creature drill 38-53k
env-fps; SMP humanoid (4096 envs, Newton) ~17k fps. A 4-creature+ball 2v2
world is estimated 8-15k env-fps here (×4 agent-steps per env-step); a 4090 is
roughly 1.5-2x this card.

### Option A — Ant soccer (validate pipeline end-to-end on DeepMind ants, then swap creatures in)

The ant is 8-DOF — the same complexity class as our rower, so drill costs
mirror ours (~1 GPU-hour per drill run, a few runs per drill).

| Budget | What it buys | Wall-clock picture |
|---|---|---|
| **$100** | The whole thing on 1× 4090: 4 drills incl. retries (~$10), warp 2v2 env bring-up experiments (~$15), self-play to ~2-5B agent-steps with warm starts (~$30-50) | ~2-3 weeks calendar at hobby pace; GPU cost is not the binding constraint |
| **$500** | Same + 2-4 GPUs in parallel: population/self-play variants side by side, ant AND our creatures simultaneously | ~1 week calendar |
| **$1000** | Add 1× A100/H100 for the big self-play runs (3-5x faster per run) + parallel 4090s for drills | Days, limited by engineering not compute |

### Option B — Reproduce "Emergent Coordination Through Competition" (Liu et al., ICLR 2019) at modern GPU speeds

Their setup, from the paper: 2v2 MuJoCo soccer; bodies are a sphere + 2 fixed
arms + box head with a **3-dim action space**; 93-dim obs; ≤45 s matches;
**inter-player contacts disabled**; PBT population of 32, 4 sampled per match.
Their timescales in *agent* steps: shaping-reward greed until ~5B, coordination
develops 5-20B, headline comparisons at 22-35B, final tournaments at **5e10
per agent** — DeepMind-scale distributed compute.

The key asymmetry: their bodies are far *simpler* than what our warp stack
already simulates (3-DoF spheres, no inter-player contact). A warp port should
comfortably exceed 100-300k env-fps on one 4090 (×4 = 0.4-1.2M agent-steps/s).

| Milestone (per-agent steps, small population ~8) | Env-steps needed | 1× 4090 wall-clock | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ball-chasing → shaping plateau (~5B) | ~10B | ~9-28 h | $3-10 |
| Clear coordination signals (~20B) | ~40B | 1.5-4.5 days | $12-37 |
| Paper-headline scale (~35B) | ~70B | 3-8 days | $25-65 |

So: **the qualitative 2019 result is reproducible for tens of dollars** on a
warp port — the cost is the ~days of engineering to port the env + a PBT-lite
loop, not the GPU. Their 5e10-per-agent tournament scale is unnecessary for
our purposes.

**Bottom line:** at this project's scale, compute under $100 is effectively
unconstrained; every tier above that buys parallelism and wall-clock, not
capability. Engineering time is the bottleneck in all scenarios.

## Parallel research track (separate effort)

The paper-worthy direction is **CompeteEvo → multi-agent**: co-evolving
morphology + control in competitive multi-agent settings, eventually for
multi-robot system design (sim-only initially). That work happens in a
separate chat/GPU and does not gate the sprint. This repo's contribution to it
is the evolved-creature tooling and the warp training stack.
