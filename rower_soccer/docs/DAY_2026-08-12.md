# 2026-08-12 — the day the CompetEvo diagnosis reversed

*Companion to `NIGHT_2026-08-11.md`. Same rule: every number here is measured in
this session or it says it is not.*

## The one-paragraph version

Yesterday's leading explanation for D2's win-rate gap was physics — our ants were
supposedly falling over more than theirs. Two measurements killed it. Counting
how games actually end showed our fall rate is **identical** to theirs (32%) and
that the gap is entirely goals-versus-timeouts. Then a cross-evaluation — their
trained weights loaded into our environment — showed their policy keeps **84% of
its goal rate** in our simulator. The environment costs 6.5 points; our training
costs 29.3. Physics is demoted to a minority term and the suspects are now all in
the optimizer, three of which are fixed and under test.

## D2 — CompetEvo

### 1. How the games end (both sides, checkpoint epoch 107)

| ending | theirs (336 games, 7 seeds) | ours (381 games) |
|---|---|---|
| reached the goal | 42.6% | 6.6% |
| **fell over** | **32.1%** | **31.8%** |
| ran out of time | 25.3% | 61.7% |

`NIGHT_2026-08-11.md:342` had inferred "~98.7% terminate early WITHOUT scoring —
falls" from a 1.3% goal rate. The premise was right and the last word was not:
the env has three exits and only two were counted. Falls are a third of endings,
not all of them, and they match the reference to 0.3 points.

### 2. Their policy in our env

| | their policy, their env | their policy, **our env** | our policy, our env |
|---|---|---|---|
| reached the goal | 41.3% | **34.8%** | 5.5% |
| fell over | 34.0% | 34.0% | 39.3% |
| travel toward goal | 3.15 m | 3.89 m | 2.69 m |

The weight remap is one-to-one and `remap()` raises on any unmapped tensor, so a
silent partial load cannot masquerade as a successful one.

### 3. What is now suspected, and what has been fixed

All four candidates are in the optimizer or the normalizer. Three are fixed:

* **`RunningNorm` clipped at 10; theirs clips at 5** and no call site of theirs
  overrides it. Found by this cross-evaluation. Every parity gate drives the
  ENV; this lives in the policy, so nothing existing could have caught it. 0.46%
  of control-observation components land beyond 5 sigma.
* **`l2_reg` at half strength.** Theirs adds `sum(p^2) * l2_reg` to the value
  loss, gradient `2 * l2_reg * p`; Adam's non-decoupled `weight_decay`
  contributes `l2_reg * p`. Now in the loss.
* **The grad clip coupled the two networks.** Theirs clips
  `policy_net.parameters()` only; clipping the joint backward rescaled the
  policy gradient by a norm that included the critic's.
* Still open: the eval protocol (their 10,000 steps over 10 workers vs our 64
  full episodes). It affects measurement, not training.

Self-play gate after the changes: 11/11.

### 4. In flight

`runs/competevo_port/m2e_fixed` — the corrected 2e re-run, 200 epochs at ~70
s/epoch. It starts bit-identical to the original at epoch 1 and diverges from
epoch 2, which is the change taking effect and nothing else moving. **The number
to watch is the goal rate: 5.5% now, 41.3% for theirs.**

## D3 — Transform2Act

### The port map (3b) and the playground (3c)

`TRANSFORM2ACT_PORT_MAP.md` maps the three-stage episode, the per-node graph
observation, the three policy towers and the critic. Three claims in it are
measured rather than reasoned:

* **float64 is load-bearing only for their log-prob reduction.** They `cumsum`
  the whole batch and difference at graph boundaries — cancellation by
  construction. fp32 that way is 0.18% off; fp32 with `index_add` is 2e-5.
* **A dense masked GraphConv matches PyG to 0.0 max error in fp32.**
* **Batching `IndexLinear` is a 0.17x SLOWDOWN in one real regime.** The loop
  costs one kernel per distinct index; the batched gather materialises an
  `[n, out, in]` weight tensor.
* **74% of hopper's wall-clock is rollout**, 26% is the update, so a
  physics-only port tops out near 3.8x. Not the raw env-step ratio — that is the
  mistake D2 made (19x raw, 3x real).

### The topology census (11)

| policy | distinct topologies / 200 designs | distinct `body_index` |
|---|---|---|
| untrained (worst case) | 21 | 12 |
| epoch 100 / 200 | 2 (199 of one) | 7–8 |

Both open port decisions settle the simple way: **group worlds by topology**
(exact, a handful of compiles) rather than compile a superset and mask, and
**keep `IndexLinear`'s loop**.

Also recorded, about the method rather than the port: **by epoch 100 the skeleton
stage has stopped exploring** while `exec_R_eps` climbs from 1,376 to 3,874 by
epoch 425. The back three quarters of the run is attribute tuning and control,
not body plan. One task, one seed — an observation, not a claim.

### The dense policy (3d step 1)

`t2a_port/dense_policy.py`, gated at **0.00e+00 max difference** against theirs
on real observations at all three stages, and at **1.44e-15** for a G=30 batch
against the same graphs run one at a time.

Writing the gate corrected two things:

* The port map warned that adjacency direction is easy to get wrong. It is not a
  hazard: `get_gnn_edges` emits both directions, so the adjacency is symmetric.
  Found by writing a control that could never fail and noticing that it did not.
* A negative control on a discrete head must read the head, not the action. The
  skeleton stage ends in an argmax, which absorbed any perturbation that left
  the winner winning: 42/60 became 60/60 on the pre-argmax output.

## D1

* **Re-scored the pinned checkpoints.** Nothing needs re-pinning — every dribble
  and shoot candidate is within 2 combined SEM. The old one-world selector
  picked arbitrarily from a genuinely indistinguishable set.
* **Dribble scores 0.847 deterministically**, against the 0.605 quoted from
  training-monitor lines. Different measurements; never compare them.
* **Dribble plateaued by ~256M steps** — its 1.36B-step checkpoint is inside the
  error bar of its 255M one.
* **The 1e gate was re-run independently** (12/12, reproducing the agent's) and
  the render was looked at. The render is where the gap was: all four ants were
  the same colour, so the 2v2 video this direction is aimed at could not show
  which team was which. Now blue/orange by team, with a bitwise-inertness check.
* `monitor/fps_now` added to six trainers. `monitor/fps` is a lifetime average
  and keeps reporting a slow patch forever, which is what made v12 read as a
  9k-fps run while it was doing 13.1k.

## Hardware, since it keeps coming up

With v12, hopper and the 2e re-run all live: **GPU utilisation 9.3% mean, memory
16%, CPU 69% idle.** Validated by adding a known-heavy job and watching util go
to 76% — the meter works, the box is genuinely idle. Uncontended raw physics is
100k env-steps/s at 2,048 worlds and 264k at 8,192 (the knee; 16,384 regresses).
v12 converts 13% of that. **We are work-limited, not compute-limited.**

## Mistakes made today

| what | how it was caught |
|---|---|
| Travel measured from the post-`reset()` pose, which both envs overwrite after the design stage | 1.22 m of travel cannot coexist with a 33.6% goal rate |
| A negative control that could never fail (transposed adjacency on an undirected graph) | it passed nothing and failed nothing; noticed it never fired |
| A negative control read through an argmax | 42/60 instead of 60/60 |
| Gated the dense policy only at G=1, the case it does not exist for | noticed while writing the commit message |
