## The headline, if you read nothing else

**D2's paper-number gate is MET.** The 1v1 re-run reaches **96.9% goal / 2.4%
falls** against the reference's **96.9% / 1.0%**, on three independent eval
seeds (97.1 / 96.8 / 96.9). M2E §10 had it at 83.9% and "close to met"; the
13-point goal gap and the 14.6-point fall gap are both gone.

**But the same code, same seed and same config gave 83.9% on the old pod and
96.9% here**, differing only in the GPU. A 13-point spread between two runs of
one configuration means what is established is that the port is *capable* of
the reference's behaviour — not that it reliably reaches it — and that the §10
and §11 fall investigations were characterising one run rather than the port.
Two more seeds are running to settle it. Full statement: M2E §12.


**Transform2Act M1 is not met, and the number we were checking against was our
own.** `TRANSFORM2ACT_M1_REPRO_NOTES.md` has said since 2026-08-10 that "the
paper's 2D locomotion converges to ~4000+". That figure was never in the paper.
The paper's Figure 3 puts 2D Locomotion at **~9,000** at 50 M steps. Our
completed 1,000-epoch run reached **6,836** — about 76% of the reference, and
below the lower edge of their shaded band.

The 2026-08-12 attempt to verify the number was recorded as failing because the
PDF exceeded the fetch limit, which made it look like an open question. It would
have failed anyway: **the paper contains no results table and no sentence
anywhere quoting a numeric return.** Two tables, both hyperparameters. Every
result is a curve.

Details and the caveats that do not close the gap: `repro/TRANSFORM2ACT_M1_REPRO_NOTES.md`,
section "What the paper actually reports".

**And the released code does not implement the paper's reward.** Equation 17 is
`r_t = |p^x_{t+1} − p^x_t| / δt + 1`, with an absolute value.
`design_opt/envs/hopper.py:159` computes `(posafter - posbefore) / self.dt` — no
`abs`, and the same signed form is in `ant.py` and `swimmer.py`. For a converged
forward runner the two coincide, so this is not automatically the explanation;
where it could bite is that a morphology search rewarded on `|Δx|` can select a
fast-vibrating body that a signed reward never would, and such a body would
score far above a runner. Either the `abs` is a write-up error and the gap is
ours to close, or Figure 3 used a reward this code cannot produce. A 300-epoch
seed with the `abs` added (`hopper_gpu_abs`, opt-in flag, default off) is
running to tell them apart.

Two things the prose did not say, both found by running it:

* `--no-index` on the PyG wheel step starves `torch-sparse` of scipy;
* something later in the install drags numpy to 2.x, where torch 1.8's C
  extension dies with `_ARRAY_API not found`. A per-line pin loses that race —
  the pins belong in a **constraints file** applied to every install.

The T2A verify step asserts a real CUDA matmul and a real `torch-scatter`
kernel, not just imports: torch 1.8+cu111 ships SASS only to sm_86 and this card
is **sm_89**, so it works through PTX JIT. That is a thing to check, not assume.
It checks out.

## 2. D2 unit 2f — the 2v2 extension is built and gated

The design agent's ranked plan had steps 1–2 done. Steps 3, 4 and 5 are now
done too.

### Step 3 — observation and network plumbing

`rower_soccer/competevo_port/team_policy.py`. One decision the doc left to
implementation, and it is the whole file:

`team_scene` orders an agent's view of the others as `[teammate, opp_near,
opp_far]`. Feeding that into a widened 1v1 net puts the **teammate's** (x, y) in
the columns the 1v1 net trained to read as the **opponent's**. Nothing crashes.
The policy is simply wrong in a way that reads as "2v2 is hard".

So the policy reorders to `[opp_near, teammate, opp_far]`. With `opp_near`
first, the leading 52 columns of a 2v2 observation **are** a 1v1 observation,
and the widening reduces to "copy the leading columns, zero the trailing ones"
for the control head and the critic alike.

The payoff is larger than the test: it makes the 83.9% 1v1 pair a legal warm
start for 2f (`widen_from_1v1`, wired to `--warm-start`) instead of something to
throw away.

`gate_team_policy.py` — **11/11**, on real batched 2v2 states:

* the column permutation asserted directly against an index-valued probe, not
  inferred from behaviour;
* `mean_action`, `value` and `log_prob` identical to the source 1v1 net at
  **max |d| = 0.000e+00**, for all four agent roles — and on states where the
  teammate channels are **live**, not zeroed as the doc asked. The stronger
  claim: the widened net ignores the new channels, rather than agreeing only
  where they happen to be blank.
* three negative controls: unpermuted others block 1.6e-2, blanked near
  opponent 3.5e-3, flipped role one-hot exactly 0.

### Step 4 — single-learner smoke

`train_team_smoke.py`, 20 iterations at 512 worlds, 291 s. All five of the
doc's checks pass: 0 NaN, 0 diverged, alpha 0.999345 → 0.986893 monotone,
forward reward +0.024/agent-step, design head still moving on the last update.

There is **no new PPO code** in it. `SelfPlayPPO.collect` already flattens over
agents and is agent-count agnostic; the only real mismatch is width (env 56,
policy 58), so the adaptation is an env wrapper and the validated trainer is
used unmodified.

One check was weaker as first written: cumulative design-head drift passes for a
head that moves once and then freezes, which is the failure a plumbing bug would
actually produce. It now also records the per-iteration step.

### Step 5 — two-learner co-evolution

`CoEvoPPO` was **generalised in place** from two *agents* to two *sides*, not
forked. The doc named the three 2-agent-shaped places (the `n_agents == 2`
assert, `_LaneEnv`, the `act[w, e]` indexing); all three now go through
`team_lanes`, which is `[[0], [1]]` at two agents and `[[0, 2], [1, 3]]` at
four. So 1v1 is the L = 1 case of the same code, and `tests/test_selfplay.py`
is the regression that says so: **15/15 before the change and 15/15 after**.

`gate_team_selfplay.py` — **13/13** — checks only what L > 1 creates, and every
one of these would train fine if it were wrong:

* **a world plays a whole past TEAM**, not two independently sampled halves of
  two different ones (doc §6). Slots pinned to distinct constants; a world's two
  opponent lanes agree to 0.000e+00, and the constants span 1.0–14.0 so the
  check can fail.
* **role follows the lane** — learner e's back-agent buffer row carries the BACK
  one-hot, with the lane swap as the negative control. Get this wrong and both
  teammates are the same agent twice, which is the exact failure 2f exists to
  avoid.
* independence at team scale: 24 tensors, 0 moved, against 17 nonzero grads on
  the other learner.
* ring lag 5.75 against epoch/4 = 6.00 at delta 0.5, 0 clamped, 0 diverged.

### The curriculum lines up with the 1v1 control by accident, and it is worth knowing

2v2 at 512 worlds is **102,400** ego agent-steps per iteration; the 1v1 run at
1,000 worlds is **100,000**. Within 2.4%, so `alpha` is at the same place at the
same epoch in both, and the control comparison is not confounded by the
exploration curriculum being at a different point.

### The 1v1 re-run reproduces the optimizer fixes on new hardware

At **epoch 160** it already exceeds the figure the old pod recorded at epoch
200, scored on the same protocol (`score_policies.py`, 372 mean-action games):

| | goal | fell | timeout | length | win, summed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| recorded, old pod @200 | 83.9% | 15.6% | 0.0% | 175.7 | 0.839 |
| **re-run, new pod @160** | **87.9%** | **11.0%** | 0.0% | 212.7 | **0.879** |
| their reference @200 | 96.9% | 1.0% | 0.0% | 170.0 | 0.969 |

Mid-run this looked like a *failure* to reproduce — at epoch 100 the logged
win rate was 0.141 against the recorded 0.395, which reads as 2.8x behind.
It was eval noise on 64 worlds against a steep curve; by 128 it was 0.712
against a recorded 0.685 at 121. **A single epoch's eval on 64 worlds is not
a measurement**, and comparing two runs at one epoch each was the wrong test.
The scored figure above, on 372 games, is.

Note this is a REPLICATE, not a reproduction: same seed, different GPU, so
warp's kernel scheduling differs and the trajectories were never going to be
identical.

### D3's two design decisions re-confirmed on the rebuilt stack

`gate_dense_policy.py` reads **8/8** again (it needed unpinning from the
deleted `checkpoint=400`; it now defaults to the newest present). Actions
still agree with theirs at 0.00e+00 / 2.78e-17 / 1.25e-15 across the three
stages — across a venv rebuilt from a script, torch 1.8+cu111 reaching sm_89
through PTX JIT, and a different card.

`topology_census.py` on the fresh checkpoint: **8** distinct topologies when
sampling, **1** at mean action, and **7-9** distinct `body_index` values. Both
port-map decisions hold — group by topology rather than mask a superset, and
keep `IndexLinear`'s Python loop, which wins at this few indices.

## 4. Open decisions for the user

   first-crossing rule the back agent's reach rate is 0.000–0.005 against the
   front pair's 0.38–0.60 — it is a spectator. The doc recommends accepting
   that and letting the sparse team reward find interference, which matches
   what you said you were hoping to see ("coordinating to flip over the
   opponents and render them useless, and then passing them by"). The `t2v2_cold`
   run in flight takes that option. The alternatives held in reserve are a
   y-gated goal or a shorter `back_x`.
2. **Whether M1 is worth chasing.** Closing 6,836 → ~9,000 is a real research
   task, not a bug hunt. A defensible answer needs 3–5 seeds at ~16 h each.
3. **Whether the CompetEvo reference should run to epoch 346** (~26 h) or stop
   at 200 (~15 h), which is where the comparison is.
