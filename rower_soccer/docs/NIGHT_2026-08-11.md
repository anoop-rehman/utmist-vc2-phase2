## The big finding: the kick drill never paid the ant to kick

Six arms across four hypotheses all failed, and the cause turned out to be a
decision recorded in our own design notes. Section 2 forced `w_strike = 0` under
the timed reward, arguing "power is a consequence of the deadline". It isn't:
**arrival is paid once, at T; the approach shaping is paid every step.** Walking
at the ball outweighs where the ball ends up.

Measured on v9, 2703 closed segments, per-segment peak ball speed:

| | |
|---|---|
| median | **0.00 m/s** |
| never really struck (<1 m/s) | **63.6%** |
| in the 6-9 m/s useful band | 1.3% |
| overshoot | 0.0% |

Not striking is an *attractor*, which is why every timed arm **declined** rather
than plateaued (v6: 0.1288 → 0.0974 over 100M). With nothing striking, aim was
unconstrained and drifted to *worse than chance*.

**The decoder result is real but narrower than I first said.** Unfreezing the
`follow` decoder is the only thing that has ever moved aim off the random
baseline — v9 74.1° against v8's 134.2° at three times the training, with
training time excluded in the strongest way (frozen arms get *worse* with more
steps). But an arm that reaches useful power in 1.3% of segments cannot express
good aim as an outcome. **Aim was necessary; a reason to strike is also
necessary; no arm has ever had both.**

### Decision 1 — the NPMP fork

v9 breaks the shared-decoder arrangement by construction. If a directed strike
is outside what a `follow`-trained decoder can represent, the design can't hold
as-is:

- **Train the decoder on a striking task too** — keeps shared-`z`; costs a
  redesign of the decoder stage and a re-run of everything downstream.
- **Per-skill decoders** — cheapest; discards the shared-`z` the pipeline is
  built on, so the 2v2 controller switches between four unrelated controllers
  rather than steering one.
- **Unfreeze during drills with a KL anchor to the follow decoder** — middle
  path, bounds gait drift, untested here.

### Decision 2 — restore `w_strike` under `timed`?

Recommended, not launched. It reverses a documented decision, and a seventh arm
before the fork above is resolved risks answering a question about a pipeline
we may be about to change.

## Update: D2 2e is validating, and the collapse reproduces

*Added later the same day, run in progress at epoch ~54 of a ~113-epoch budget.*

Their own CPU run of this exact config is **alive on this pod at epoch 180+**
(PID 2846036), with per-epoch eval reward and win rate from epoch 0. That is a
far better reference than the paper figures the plan called for — same config,
same machine, epoch for epoch. Comparison so far:

| epoch | THEIRS eval R | OURS eval R |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 428.0 / 428.5 | 509.9 / 496.4 |
| 24 | 405.8 / 433.6 | 484.3 / 524.2 |
| 36 | 444.8 / 425.2 | 394.8 / 464.3 |
| 42 | **203.6 / 187.7** | **213.0 / 229.3** |

**The characteristic collapse reproduces on the same schedule.** Both runs hold

goes negative through 54-72 and recovers (52/79 at 78, 271/271 at 90) with win

Note the collapse is NOT the curriculum: alpha is still ~0.95 at epoch 50, so
the dense move-to-goal term is at nearly full weight. It is the agents learning
to block each other, which is also why win rates start appearing afterwards.

### The "ours wins at epoch 46, theirs at 89" headline is an artifact

`compare_curves` prints that, and it is misleading. Our first nonzero win rate
is **0.00568** — about one win in 176 games, 0.57%. Their eval runs 10 workers,
so their smallest resolvable nonzero is **0.10**. A 0.57% win rate is invisible
to their instrument by construction.

We are not learning to win 43 epochs earlier; we can SEE a rare win they cannot.
The apples-to-apples question is the epoch at which our win rate crosses 0.10,
their resolution floor, and that is the number any comparison should quote.

This is precisely why the mapping table's three declared mismatches matter
(`l2_reg` at half strength, grad-clip scope, and `eval_batch_size`). The third
one turned out to bite exactly where it would mislead — a validation is only as
good as its declared mismatches, and this is the argument for writing them down
before looking at results.

### 2e trough update (epoch 71)

Both runs bottom in the same window and plateau there:

| epoch | THEIRS | OURS |
|---|---|---|

Same shape, with ours a consistent ~+65 above theirs through the trough. That

What the collapse physically IS: episode length falls 500 → 43 steps while

*Epoch 87 of a ~107-epoch budget.*

|---|---|---|

| 66 (trough) | -13.8 / -4.8 | 58.7 / 51.2 |

Both turn upward at **epoch 72** and climb through 78 and 84. The ~+65 offset
noted in the trough has **closed**: at epoch 84 their two-agent mean is 147.6
against ours at 144.9.

Episode length confirms this is behaviour, not scoring: 43 → 51 → 86 → 106

falling over and start staying up, on the same schedule as the reference.

**This is the substance of M2.** An independent GPU reimplementation reproducing
a NON-MONOTONE trajectory — hold ~450, crater at 42, trough through 66, recover
from 72, converge by 84 — is much stronger evidence of faithfulness than
matching a single final number. A wrong implementation has many ways to be
monotone and very few ways to reproduce a collapse and recovery at the right
epochs.

Still outstanding for a complete 2e: the epoch at which OUR win rate crosses
0.10 (their resolution floor), which needs the run to reach ~96-107 where theirs
first shows 0.17. And the two declared mismatches remain unquantified as to
effect (`l2_reg` at half strength, grad-clip scope) — the trough offset is the
best evidence we have that they do something, and it washed out by epoch 84.

### 2e final: epoch 90 agrees to ~2%, and the win-rate axis is too noisy to compare

| epoch | THEIRS | OURS |
|---|---|---|
| 84 | 201.2 / 94.0 | 141.4 / 148.4 |
| **90** | **270.9 / 270.9** | **275.2 / 283.0** |
| 96 | 119.8 / 276.0 | 327.6 / 349.0 |

**Epoch 90 is the strongest single point in the comparison**: the two
implementations agree to within ~2%, having been ~65 apart in the trough twenty
epochs earlier. Episode length recovers in step (43 → 230 → 303) with games per
eval falling 689 → 104 → 79.

**On win rate, the honest answer is that the reference cannot resolve the
question.** Ours reads 0.03 at epoch 90 and 0.01 at 96; theirs 0.17 at 96. That
looks like a gap, and it is not one that survives arithmetic: their 0.17 is
about 1.7 wins out of TEN games (binomial 1 sigma ~ +/-0.12), ours about 1 win
out of 79. The two are ~1.3 sigma apart — indistinguishable in either
direction. A 10-game eval simply cannot measure a win rate to better than
+/-10 points, so no win-rate comparison at this scale can be conclusive,
whichever way it comes out.

That is the third declared mismatch (`eval_batch_size`) determining what the
milestone can and cannot claim, and it is worth stating as a limit on the
protocol rather than a property of the port: **2e can validate the reward
trajectory and cannot validate the win-rate onset.** Doing the latter would need
their eval widened, which is a change to their code, not ours.

### 2e FINAL — corrected. The win rate does NOT match, and I got this wrong.

*The run finished: 107 epochs in 257 min, 144 s/epoch, 0 diverged over 10.7M ego
transitions. Full detail in `competevo_port/M2E_VALIDATION.md`.*

**Retraction.** The section above says the win-rate axis "cannot be compared"
because the reference's 10-game eval is too noisy. That was wrong, and wrong by
the exact error this document already warns about: I compared a SINGLE epoch
(96) instead of aggregating. Over epochs 78-106 the comparison is decisive:

| | agent 0 | agent 1 |
|---|---|---|
| ours (15 evals, 2,017 games) | 0.0019 | **0.0143** |
| theirs | 0.0000 | **0.1667** |

A ~12x gap, roughly 10 sigma, and **widening** — at epoch 106 ours is 0.058
against their 0.50. The port reproduces their LEARNING and not their SCORING.

**What does match, and it is a lot.** Training reward end to end: ours
-1089 → +27, theirs -1047 → +38. Eval-reward correlation over the 54
overlapping epochs: **r = +0.936 / +0.965**. Same three-phase shape at the same
epochs, matching eval episode lengths through the trough (ours 43/43/48, theirs
43/44/46). And in BOTH runs agent 0 never wins a single eval while agent 1 does
— an asymmetry nobody designed in.

**My attribution of the reward offset was also wrong.** I blamed `l2_reg` and
grad-clip scope. The offset is present at EPOCH 0, before any training, so it
cannot be either: with both ants standing still for 500 steps, THEIR ant slides
1.08 m backwards and ours does not (theirs 428 vs ours 501 +/- 10 over 8 seeds).
That is a physics difference (fp32+Newton vs float64+PGS), and it is the leading
suspect for the win-rate gap too — if our ants cover ground differently they
reach x = +/-4 less often. Untested; the cheap probe is logging goal-vs-fall
episode endings on both sides.

**The paper-number gate is NOT met.** 2e validates the reward trajectory and
fails on win rate.

### Two real bugs the 2e work found, and one invalidates earlier claims

1. **Control cost was billed on the CLIPPED action.** Theirs charges
   `0.5 * sum(a^2)` on the raw policy output and lets MuJoCo clamp the torque
   separately. At `log_std = 0` that is 4.0/step against our 2.1 — and while
   alpha ~ 1, the control cost IS the reward. Measured: our rollouts ran at
   -1.10/step, theirs implies -3.0, after the fix -3.00. **Every parity gate
   missed it** because they drive `terms()` directly and only `step()`'s clamp
   was wrong. This invalidates the reward that all stage-1/2/3 learning claims
   were measured against.
2. The design step leaked `dense`/`parse` into the info dict (theirs returns
   0/0), paying a spurious +1 per episode.

Stage-3 gate re-run after both fixes: 11/11.

### Where the win-rate gap probably lives (from logged data, no new probe)

Our eval episode length against the 500-step cap, with the goal rate:

| epoch | length | goals | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-24 | 500 (cap) | 0% | upright, never reaches the goal |
| 36-72 | 411 → **43** | 0% | falling almost immediately |
| 84-96 | 106 → **303** | 0.9 → 1.3% | recovering, goals appearing |

At epoch 96 our episodes end at 303 of 500 with a 1.3% goal rate, so ~98.7%
terminate early WITHOUT scoring — falls. Theirs score 17% of the time.

That is consistent with the epoch-0 physics difference (their ant slides 1.08 m
backwards over 500 idle steps, ours does not): a contact/friction discrepancy
would plausibly change both how readily ants fall and how far they travel before
they do.

**Not established**, and this is the honest boundary: separating "ours falls
sooner" from "ours travels slower" needs episode endings instrumented on both
sides — theirs logs win rate but not a goal/fall/timeout breakdown. That is the
next concrete step for anyone picking D2 back up, and it is a physics question,
not an RL one.
